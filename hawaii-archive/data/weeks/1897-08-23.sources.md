This ledger extends `1897-09-06.sources.md`. The September 6–12 fixture and ledger remain accepted evidence for the later part of the window; do not duplicate those 47 records merely to widen the public chronology.

## Governing rule

Follow historical attention. Do not manufacture balance. Preserve repetition when repetition itself shows what people cared about. Preserve multiple voices and places. Add ordinary life wherever the archive gives it to us. Preserve the lag between something happening and people learning about it.

The widened public window is **August 23–September 12, 1897**. Empty or thin days are allowed. Do not manufacture posts to make every date look equally busy.

## Grounded lead-up now surfaced

### September 1 — Ebbitt House coordination letter

Primary historical object: a September 1, 1897 letter from Ebbitt House in Washington, D.C., addressed to James Keauiluna Kaulia and later published by ʻAʻima Nāwahī. Noenoe K. Silva identifies the letter as being from Joseph Heleluhe on behalf of Liliʻuokalani and quotes the Hawaiian text in *Aloha Betrayed*.

Current access / transcription authority:
- https://dokumen.pub/aloha-betrayed-native-hawaiian-resistance-to-american-colonialism-9780822386223.html

Useful source-facing phrases include:
- `e kuka like la olua` — the two leaders are directed to confer together.
- `e kahea ia la hoi ka Papa hooko a me ka Aha Kuwaena o na Hui a elua` — the executive/central leadership of the two organizations is to be convened.
- `hoopau i na manao kuee mawaena o oukou` — end the conflicts among all of you.

The surrounding quoted passage also directs the joint leadership meeting to settle the heading for the lāhui's protest of annexation and have aloha ʻāina makaʻāinana sign under it. The letter ends with an instruction to keep women's signatures on pages marked `Hui Aloha Aina o na Wahine`.

Do not infer a newspaper publication date for this letter until the direct *Ke Aloha Aina* issue is resolved. September 1 is the correspondence/event date.

## High-value leads not yet promoted to feed posts

### September 2 — Kalaupapa celebrates Liliʻuokalani

National Park Service historical documentation states that Robert M. Kaaoao, president of the Kalaupapa branch of Hui Aloha ʻĀina, organized a September 2 celebration for Liliʻuokalani that included speeches plus boating, swimming, running, horse/donkey races, pole-climbing, and apple-eating contests. About nine days later, roughly seven hundred Kalaupapa residents signed the anti-annexation petition.

Research authority:
- https://npshistory.com/publications/kala/nr-kalaupapa-leprosy-settlement-new.pdf

This is an unusually valuable politics-plus-ordinary-life candidate. Keep it in research until source-facing Hawaiian from the contemporary newspaper/report is recovered; the current text-item contract should not invent a Hawaiian field from an English secondary description.

### August 21 — unity / peace mele immediately before the window

*Ke Aloha Aina*, August 21, 1897, printed Samuel K. Kamakaia's `NAI WALE NO OUKOU AO'E PAU`, including the repeated instruction `E malama i ka maluhia` and unity language. It falls two days before the chosen August 23 public window, so it is context rather than a current post unless the window is intentionally widened again.

Research access:
- https://nupepa-hawaii.com/category/mele-2/page/51/

English-language papers can identify events, names, ships, businesses, recreation, and other leads worth chasing back into Hawaiian-language sources. They are discovery evidence, not a substitute for source-facing Hawaiian in the current text contract.

## Extraction priorities

Do not search for predetermined proportions. Look for:
- annexation treaty discussion, rumors, warnings, editorials, meetings, letters, and organizing;
- visible disagreement or attempts to coordinate among Hui Aloha ʻĀina, Hui Kālaiʻāina, and women's leadership;
- movement of people and information between Washington, Honolulu, and the islands;
- ships, arrivals/departures, roads, commerce, weather, recreation, celebrations, deaths, marriages, court notices, advertisements, and odd local happenings when the papers themselves give them attention;
- explicit delays between event, correspondence, publication, circulation, and receipt.

## Evidence discipline

- Preserve exact historical date type: an event date is not automatically a publication date.
- An English report can be a research lead without becoming a Hawaiian-language feed item.
- A secondary scholarly transcription may support a `supported` pilot record while direct issue verification remains queued.
- Do not assign a day to an undated late-August/early-September letter merely because it fits the window.
- Do not split one source paragraph into many posts unless each fragment carries a materially different historical/social move.
