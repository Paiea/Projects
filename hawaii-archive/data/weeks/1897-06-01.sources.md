# Hawaiʻi Archive source ledger — June 1–September 12, 1897

This ledger governs the widened text window. It extends the accepted August 23–September 12 window rather than duplicating it.

## Chronology guard

- **June 16, 1897:** an annexation treaty was signed in Washington by representatives of the United States and the Republic of Hawaiʻi.
- **June 17, 1897:** Liliʻuokalani delivered a formal protest against ratification.
- **1897 is an annexation crisis, not completed U.S. annexation.** Formal U.S. annexation followed the Newlands Resolution in 1898, with sovereignty transferred August 12, 1898.
- Feed wording must therefore say treaty, proposed annexation, annexation crisis, resistance, ratification, or similar evidence-bounded language. Do not describe Hawaiʻi as having become U.S. territory in June 1897.

Authority lanes:

  https://libweb.hawaii.edu/digicoll/annexation/protest/liliu5.php
- *Ke Aloha Aina*, July 10, 1897, p. 6, Hawaiian transcription of the protest as surfaced by Nūpepa Hawaiʻi:
  https://nupepa-hawaii.com/2014/01/28/the-queens-protest-to-treaty-1897/
- U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian, for treaty / formal-annexation chronology:
  https://history.state.gov/milestones/1866-1898/hawaii

Accepted atomic signals in `1897-06-01.json`:

1. June 16 treaty context, surfaced through Liliʻuokalani's documented reaction.
2. June 17 formal protest of ratification.
3. Treaty characterized as a wrong against Native Hawaiian and part-Hawaiian people.
4. Lack of consultation / consent as a central protest argument.
5. Direct request to withdraw the treaty and refuse ratification.

Information-time rule:

The protest was made June 17 in Washington. *Ke Aloha Aina* printed the Hawaiian protest on July 10. Preserve that 23-day event-to-publication gap rather than making the information network behave like modern social media.
